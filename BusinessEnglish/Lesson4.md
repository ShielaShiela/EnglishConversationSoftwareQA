## 🧵 Part 1: Key Vocabulary

### Materials
| Term | Definition | Example Sentence |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Upper | The part of the shoe above the sole | "The upper is made of full-grain leather." |
| Outsole | The bottom layer that contacts the ground | "We're using a rubber outsole for better grip." |
| Midsole | The cushioning layer between outsole and upper | "The midsole uses EVA foam for shock absorption." |
| Lining | The interior fabric layer | "The lining is breathable mesh." |
| Insole / Footbed | The inner cushioning you stand on | "This insole is removable and orthopaedic-friendly." |
| Toe box | The front section covering the toes | "The toe box has extra reinforcement." |
| Counter | The stiffener at the heel | "The heel counter gives structure and support." |
| Welt | The strip connecting upper to sole | "It uses a Goodyear welt construction." |

### Common Materials
| Material | Properties | Typical Use |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Full-grain leather | Durable, premium, ages well | Dress shoes, boots |
| Split leather | Lower grade, less breathable | Budget casual shoes |
| PU (Polyurethane) | Lightweight, synthetic | Fashion sneakers |
| TPR (Thermoplastic Rubber) | Flexible, slip-resistant | Casual outsoles |
| EVA (Ethylene Vinyl Acetate) | Lightweight, cushioning | Midsoles, sandals |
| Canvas | Breathable, casual | Sneakers, espadrilles |
| Mesh | Breathable, lightweight | Athletic shoes |
| Suede | Soft, textured look | Loafers, boots |

## 💬 Part 2: Small Talk — Before the Meeting

> Small talk builds rapport and trust. It often happens before diving into product discussions.

---

### Scenario A: Meeting a Supplier for the First Time

**Setting:** A costing officer meets a new supplier representative at the factory.

---

**Maria (Costing Officer):** Hi! You must be Kevin. I'm Maria, the costing officer from Stride & Co. Nice to finally meet you in person!

**Kevin (Supplier Rep):** Hi Maria! Yes, that's me. Great to meet you too. How was your trip here?

**Maria:** It was good, thank you! The flight was a bit long, but the weather here is lovely. Much warmer than back home!

**Kevin:** Ha, yes — this time of year it's always warm. Did you get a chance to rest?

**Maria:** Just a quick rest last night. I'm excited to see the factory today, actually. I've heard a lot of good things.

**Kevin:** We're glad to have you. Can I get you some coffee or water before we start?

**Maria:** Coffee would be great, thank you. I'd love to see the sample room first if that's okay.

**Kevin:** Of course! Right this way.

---

### 💡 Small Talk Tips
- Ask about the other person's trip or commute
- Comment on the weather or location
- Express genuine interest in the factory or product
- Accept offers of refreshment — it builds warmth
- Keep it brief (2–4 exchanges) before moving to business

## 👟 Part 3: Product Description — Sample Conversations

---

### Scenario C: Costing Officer Explains a Product to a New Team Member

**Setting:** An office. Maria is briefing a new junior costing officer, Tom, on a shoe sample.

---

**Tom:** Maria, can you walk me through this sample? I'm not too familiar with the construction yet.

**Maria:** Sure! So this is a men's casual lace-up. Let's start from the bottom. The **outsole** is made of **rubber** — specifically a **TPR compound** — which gives it good grip and flexibility.

**Tom:** And that middle layer?

**Maria:** That's the **midsole**. It's **EVA foam**. It's light and provides cushioning. For a casual shoe like this, we don't need a thick midsole — maybe 10mm.

**Tom:** Okay. And what about the top part?

**Maria:** The **upper** — that's the main body of the shoe. This one uses **split leather** on the toe and side panels, with a **mesh lining** inside for breathability.

**Tom:** Is split leather common?

**Maria:** It depends on the price point. For budget to mid-range, yes. For premium lines, we'd use **full-grain leather** or even **suede**. Split leather helps keep the cost down.

**Tom:** Got it. And what about this part at the back?

**Maria:** That's the **heel counter** — it's a rigid piece inside the heel to keep the shape and give support. You won't always see it, but it's always there.

**Tom:** Makes sense. So how does all this affect the costing?

**Maria:** Great question. Each component has a material cost and a labor cost. The upper materials are usually the biggest cost driver — maybe 35 to 45 percent of total material cost. The outsole and midsole come next.

### Scenario D: Costing Officer Presents to a Client

**Setting:** A video call between a costing officer (Lisa) and a client (David) reviewing a new shoe design.

---

**David:** Lisa, thanks for sending over the cost sheet. Before we go through the numbers, can you give me a quick overview of the construction?

**Lisa:** Of course, David. So this is the women's walking shoe — Style WK-209. Let me describe the key components.

**David:** Perfect, go ahead.

**Lisa:** The **upper** is a combination of **genuine leather** at the toe cap and heel, with **breathable mesh** panels on the sides and tongue. This gives a clean, sporty look while keeping the shoe lightweight.

**David:** And the sole?

**Lisa:** The **outsole** is a **rubber compound** — we're using a carbon rubber blend at the heel for durability, and a lighter blown rubber at the forefoot. The **midsole** is **PU foam**, which provides more rebound than standard EVA. Great for all-day wear.

**David:** I like that. What about the interior?

**Lisa:** Inside, we have a **moisture-wicking textile lining** and a **removable cushion insole** with memory foam. That's actually one of the premium touches on this style.

**David:** That sounds good for the comfort angle. Now, what's driving the cost up from the last version?

**Lisa:** Mainly two things: the **leather components** at the toe and heel, and the **memory foam insole**. If we substitute the leather with a high-quality **PU synthetic**, we can bring the FOB down by about USD 1.20 per pair without affecting the look significantly.

**David:** Interesting. Can you send over a cost comparison between the two options?

**Lisa:** Absolutely. I'll have it to you by end of day.

### Scenario E: Discussing a Design Change with the Product Team

**Setting:** A product development meeting. The costing officer (Carlos) raises a concern about a material change.

---

**Designer (Priya):** So for the new boot, we were thinking of switching from the **PU outsole** to a **genuine rubber outsole**. What do you think, Carlos?

**Carlos:** Design-wise, it looks great. But from a costing perspective, rubber outsoles are heavier and more expensive. Can I ask — is this for the premium line?

**Priya:** Yes, it's the Heritage Boot. We want it to feel more substantial.

**Carlos:** That makes sense then. Genuine rubber will add about **USD 0.80 to USD 1.20** per pair on the outsole alone, depending on the thickness and compound. Plus the weight adds to shipping costs.

**Priya:** Is there a middle ground?

**Carlos:** We could look at a **TPR outsole with a leather texture emboss** — it mimics the look of a premium sole but costs less. Or, we keep genuine rubber but simplify the upper construction to balance the overall cost.

**Priya:** I like the second idea. What if we use a **split suede** on the shaft instead of full suede?

**Carlos:** That could work. Split suede is roughly 30 to 40 percent cheaper than full suede. Let me run the numbers and come back to you tomorrow.

**Priya:** Perfect. Thanks, Carlos.

## 📝 Part 4: Useful Phrases for Product Description

### Describing Materials
- "The upper is made of / constructed from ___."
- "We're using ___ for the outsole because it offers ___."
- "The lining is a ___ material, which helps with ___."
- "This component accounts for roughly ___ percent of total material cost."

### Explaining Design Choices
- "The design features ___, which gives it ___."
- "We chose ___ over ___ because ___."
- "This is a premium touch that adds value to the consumer."
- "The silhouette is ___ with ___ detailing."

### Discussing Costs
- "From a costing perspective, this adds approximately ___ per pair."
- "The biggest cost driver here is ___."
- "If we substitute ___ with ___, we can reduce cost by ___."
- "The FOB price includes material, labor, and packaging."

### Asking for Clarification
- "Could you walk me through the construction?"
- "What's the spec for the midsole thickness?"
- "Is this for the premium line or the standard range?"
- "What's the target FOB for this style?"

## 📌 Quick Reference: FOB & Costing Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| FOB (Free on Board) | Price that includes production and delivery to the port |
| BOM (Bill of Materials) | Full list of materials and components |
| CMT (Cut, Make, Trim) | Labor-only costing (buyer supplies materials) |
| MOQ (Minimum Order Quantity) | Minimum units a supplier will produce per order |
| Lead time | Time from order to delivery |
| Spec sheet | Technical document with all product measurements and materials |
