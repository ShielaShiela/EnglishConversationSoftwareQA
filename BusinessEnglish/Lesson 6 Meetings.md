# Lesson 6: Participating in Meetings
**Conversational English · Workplace Context: Shoe Design Industry**  
**Duration:** 50 minutes

---

## Lesson Overview

| Section | Activity | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm-up: Small Talk | 5 min |
| 2 | Vocabulary Focus | 10 min |
| 3 | Practice Dialogues | 15 min |
| 4 | Role Play | 15 min |
| 5 | Debrief & Reflection | 5 min |

**Learning objectives:** By the end of this lesson, students will be able to join a workplace meeting discussion, use agenda-related vocabulary, politely contribute and respond to others, and ask for clarification confidently.

## Section 1 — Warm-up: Small Talk Before the Meeting *(5 min)*

> **Scene:** It's Monday morning at *SoleCraft* — a shoe design company. Before the weekly design review meeting, colleagues are chatting in the conference room.

### Small talk topics and questions

**Weekend / General**
- How was your weekend?
- Did you do anything fun?
- Is this your first meeting of the day?

**Work / Office**
- Have you seen the new collection samples?
- The conference room is so cold today, isn't it?
- Did you get a chance to review the agenda?

**Industry-Specific (Shoe Design)**
- I heard the new trainer design got great feedback!
- Have you tried the new material samples yet?
- The trade show is coming up — are you going?

### Practice prompt
> Pair up and have a **2-minute small talk conversation**. Use at least one question from any topic above.

## Section 2 — Vocabulary Focus *(10 min)*

### Key words

**Agenda** /əˈdʒen.də/
A list of topics to be discussed in a meeting, usually shared in advance.
> *"Let's go through the agenda before we start. First item is the new sole design."*

---

**Discussion** /dɪˈskʌʃ.ən/
A conversation where people share ideas and opinions about a topic.
> *"We had a great discussion about the materials for the summer collection."*

---

**Action points** /ˈæk.ʃən pɔɪnts/
Tasks that need to be done after a meeting, assigned to specific people.
> *"The action point for Maya is to contact the leather supplier by Friday."*

---

**Clarify** /ˈklær.ɪ.faɪ/
To make something clearer or easier to understand.
> *"Could you clarify what you mean by the 'revised colour palette'?"*

---

**Take the floor** /teɪk ðə flɔː/
To start speaking in a formal or group setting.
> *"Maria, would you like to take the floor and present the heel concept?"*

---

**Wrap up** /ræp ʌp/
To finish or bring a meeting or discussion to an end.
> *"Let's wrap up — we're running short on time. Any final thoughts?"*

---

### Meeting phrases by function

**Opening & starting**
- "Shall we get started?"
- "Let's kick things off."
- "First on the agenda is…"

**Joining in / adding your view**
- "I'd like to add…"
- "Building on that…"
- "If I could just jump in…"
- "From a design perspective…"

**Asking for clarification**
- "Sorry, could you repeat that?"
- "What do you mean by…?"
- "Could you clarify that point?"

**Agreeing / disagreeing politely**
- "I think that's a great idea."
- "I see your point, but…"
- "I'm not sure about that — how about…?"

> **Quick check:** Cover the definition and try to remember each word's meaning from the example sentence. Then check with your partner.

## Section 3 — Practice Dialogues *(15 min)*

### Dialogue A — Opening a Meeting

*Context: The weekly design review at SoleCraft. The team leader opens the meeting.*

---

**Alex (Team Leader):** Good morning, everyone. Shall we get started? I know we're all busy today. Does everyone have a copy of the agenda?

**Maya (Designer):** Yes, I have it. Actually, could we also add a quick update on the supplier meeting? It's pretty urgent.
*(→ Adding a point to the agenda)*

**Alex:** Of course. Let's add that at the end. Right — first on the agenda is the new sole design for the autumn collection. Maya, would you like to take the floor?

**Maya:** Sure, thanks Alex. So, we've been looking at two options — a rubber compound and a recycled EVA foam. I'd love to hear everyone's thoughts on both.

---

> **Practice tip:** Read aloud in pairs. Then try again — this time without looking at the script. Use your own words!

---

### Dialogue B — Joining a Discussion

*Context: The discussion has moved to colour choices for the new sneaker line.*

---

**Alex:** So, the current proposal is to go with neutral tones — beige, off-white, and slate grey. Any thoughts?

**Jin (Marketing):** If I could just jump in — I think neutral tones are a safe choice, but our target customers are Gen Z. They tend to go for bold colours.
*(→ Politely interrupting to add a view)*

**Maya:** Building on that — what if we kept the base neutral but added a pop-colour accent on the tongue or laces? That way we appeal to both groups.
*(→ Building on a colleague's idea)*

**Ryan (Production):** I see your point, but from a production standpoint, multiple colourways means higher costs. Could we maybe limit it to three options?
*(→ Politely disagreeing + offering a solution)*

**Alex:** Great discussion, everyone. Let's put three colourways as an action point for Ryan to cost out before next week.

---

> **Discussion task:** In groups of 3–4, continue this conversation. What colour combinations would you suggest for the sneaker? Use the phrase categories above to guide you.

---

### Dialogue C — Asking for Clarification

*Context: A junior designer isn't sure about a technical term used in the meeting.*

---

**Alex:** We need to finalise the welt construction method — whether we go with Goodyear welt or Blake stitch for the dress shoe range.

**Sofia (Junior Designer):** Sorry — could you clarify what you mean by "welt construction"? I've heard the term but I'm not totally clear on the difference.
*(→ Asking for clarification confidently)*

**Maya:** Good question. Basically, it's how the upper part of the shoe is attached to the sole. Goodyear welt is more durable and water-resistant — great for formal shoes. Blake stitch is lighter and cheaper.

**Sofia:** That makes sense, thank you. So for the autumn dress shoe range, would Goodyear welt make more sense given the weather?
*(→ Checking understanding + contributing)*

**Alex:** Exactly right, Sofia. That's a good point to note. Okay, I'll put that down as a recommendation.

---

> **Tip:** Asking for clarification is professional, not a sign of weakness! Notice how Sofia stays engaged and turns the clarification into a contribution.

## Section 4 — Role Play: Join a Meeting Discussion *(15 min)*

### Scenario A — The New Trainer Launch

**Context:** It's the quarterly product meeting at SoleCraft. The team is deciding whether to launch the new "UrbanStep" trainer in spring or wait until summer. Budget, production time, and competitor launches are all factors.

**Roles:**
- Meeting Chair — opens and manages the agenda
- Marketing Manager — wants a spring launch
- Production Lead — needs more time
- Finance Representative — concerned about budget
- Junior Designer — asks questions and clarifies

**Your goal:** Hold a 5-minute meeting. The chair must follow the agenda, everyone must speak at least once, and the team must agree on an action point by the end.

---

### Scenario B — The Sustainability Problem

**Context:** SoleCraft's biggest retail partner wants 30% of the next collection to use recycled materials. The team needs to discuss how to meet this requirement without missing the production deadline.

**Roles:**
- Team Leader
- Sustainability Coordinator
- Senior Designer
- Supplier Liaison

**Your goal:** Reach at least two action points. Use phrases for joining in, clarifying, and wrapping up.

---

### Language support — use these during role play

- "I'd like to raise a point about…"
- "Can I add something here?"
- "What exactly do you mean by…?"
- "I agree, although I'm a bit concerned about…"
- "So to summarise, we've agreed that…"
- "Let's move on to the next point."
